# **Hampura — A Reflection on Forgiveness and Inner Peace by Metta Legita**

*Hampura*, a word from Sundanese meaning *forgiveness*—*to forgive, to be forgiven*—is one of my deeply personal compositions, now released as both a live music video and on digital platforms. The tittle 'Hamper 'carries a profound meaning of forgiveness, acceptance, and inner peace.

The inspiration behind this piece began during my studies with Armenian jazz pianist and composer Vardan Ovsepian, whom I have been learning from since 2022. Through his teachings, I explored not only piano technique—especially from his book *Mirror Exercises*—but also composition and jazz harmony. Each lesson with him has been a source of new ideas and musical discovery.

This piece was born from those moments of inspiration. Often, after a class, I would sit at the piano and let my hands move freely across the keys, allowing ideas to unfold naturally. When something resonated, I would record it on my phone or sketch it down by hand. In this article, I share some of those original handwritten notes that capture the early development of *Hampura*.

![](/images/uploads/20240722-184756.jpg)

Structurally, the composition is set in 3/4 time, with a strong and expressive opening motif. As the piece evolves, the harmonic progression emerged intuitively—almost by accident—through a process of listening deeply and trusting the sound. The middle section introduces an ostinato with an irregular rhythmic feel, a technique I discovered through my studies with Vardan.

On a deeper level, *Hampura* reflects my personal journey, especially in recent years. As I have grown older, I have experienced profound spiritual processes—learning about awareness, self-understanding, and acceptance. Acceptance, in particular, has been one of the most challenging aspects, yet through it, I have found a sense of true inner freedom.

This composition holds a very special meaning for me. It is not only about music, but about letting go, forgiving, and embracing life as it is.

I am deeply grateful for the support of those closest to me and the incredible team who contributed to this work—Mathias, Bjorn, Deon, Jesse, Lewis—and most importantly, my teacher Vardan, whose guidance continues to inspire my artistic path. I share the link of the video here.
