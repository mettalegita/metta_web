# Living Room Series Vol. 1 — A Flashback

At the end of April, on 25th April, I finally held the first edition of my Living Room Series. It was something simple in form, but emotionally very meaningful to me. Several people came, sat together closely, listened carefully, and shared the evening with openness. I’m truly grateful it happened.

![](/images/uploads/img_3349.png)

For this session, I played mostly my original compositions alongside some repertoire pieces that I deeply love: *Bright Size Life*, *Falling Grace*, *Love Dance*, *Norwegian Wood*, and *Tico-Tico*. These songs have stayed with me for a long time and somehow always reconnect me with why I love music in the first place.

One of the most memorable moments of the night was performing my piece *Cikaracak*. It was the first time I had played it publicly since its release a few years ago. Before playing, I explained to the audience that the piece is inspired by the flow of water. This time, I expanded the composition further and combined it with Aram Khachaturian’s *Toccata* in the middle section, together with spontaneous arrangements and improvisations in the moment.

![](/images/uploads/img_1334.png)

During that performance, I genuinely felt something magical and transcendent happen inside me. It brought me back to memories from the pandemic years — difficult times, loneliness after my divorce, and the long process of finding myself again. Somehow, through the music, those emotions transformed into something lighter. After playing it, I felt better. 

Some people who wanted to come on the first date had to cancel for various reasons, and that naturally led me to create another session on 2nd May. What I keep reflecting on after these two sessions is this: music feels deeply alive when it is shared directly with people, no matter the size of the place or the number of listeners. There is always another dimension that appears through preparation, presence, and the exchange between performer and audience.

![](/images/uploads/img_3350.png)

This is something I want to continue exploring and developing — how we can express music more intimately for society, for human connection, and for ourselves as musicians.

I’m grateful for everyone who support especially Mathias, and for those who came, listened, and shared the space with me. Special thanks to Lewis McTie for the beautiful surprise performance of *This Love Is Over* by Ray LaMontagne. I truly love Lewis’ vocal expression and it made the evening even more memorable.

Looking forward to continuing the journey.

– Metta
